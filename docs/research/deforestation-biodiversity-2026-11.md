Status: the North Cork, Tirlán and ESB evidence (sections 2 and 3) was applied to `site/data/data.json` on 12 November 2026. Everything else below is still a proposal.

## 1. Deforestation and felling

**Illegal felling.** The Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine investigated 72 reports of alleged illegal felling in 2025. One person was prosecuted and two replanting orders were issued; the previous year's investigations gave three prosecutions and 14 replanting orders. Replanting orders are the main tool. Known convictions are individual farmers and landowners, which we do not list (privacy and the "organisations only" rule). Source: [Farmers Journal](https://www.farmersjournal.ie/over-70-investigations-into-alleged-illegal-tree-felling-891546).

**Coillte.** Fined €5,580 at Rathdrum Court in May 2003 for felling 186 Scots pine without a licence in a sensitive conservation area ([Irish Times, 16 May 2003](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/coillte-fined-5-580-for-felling-trees-without-a-licence-1.359142)). **Recommendation: do not add.** It is 23 years old and predates the Forestry Act 2014. Counting it would give Coillte the "Repeat offender" badge on the strength of a 2003 case. Coillte's current Drumwood stop-felling order and Glenaboy case already cover this ground.

**Permanent deforestation.** The Department estimated about 1,200 to 1,600 ha a year of deforestation for 2013 to 2019, from National Forest Inventory plots scaled up nationally. It says felled land must normally be replanted, but permanent removal can be allowed for wind farms and other projects ([Oireachtas PQ, 7 Dec 2023](https://www.oireachtas.ie/en/debates/question/2023-12-07/285/)). No per-company figures. **Use as context only.**

## 2. Wind farm bog slides (peat, rivers, habitats)

**ESB: Derrybrien, Co Galway (existing entry).**
- October 2003: a peat slide during construction of the 70-turbine wind farm, built by an ESB subsidiary, polluted the Owendalulleegh River. Estimates of the fish kill range from 50,000 to 100,000.
- November 2019: the Court of Justice of the EU fined Ireland €5m plus €15,000 a day, because no proper environmental impact assessment had been done before construction.
- 2022: An Bord Pleanála refused substitute consent to Gort Windfarms Ltd (an ESB subsidiary). ESB stopped generating and decided to decommission all 70 turbines.
- Sources: [Irish Times: ESB to decommission Derrybrien](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/energy-and-resources/esb-to-decommission-derrybrien-wind-farm-s-70-turbines-1.4828766), [Irish Times: fish kill](https://www.irishtimes.com/news/galway-landslide-caused-significant-fish-kill-1.1132902).
- **Proposed:** add as dated evidence to the ESB entry. **No score change**: the fine was against the State, not ESB, the outcome of the 2000s prosecution of Gort Windfarms is not confirmed, and the site is being decommissioned. Worth noting on the entry because it is the clearest Irish case of wind farm construction destroying peat and river habitat.

**Planree Ltd: Meenbog wind farm, Co Donegal (candidate new entry).**
- November 2020: a bog slide at the 19-turbine site at Cashelnavean polluted the Shruhangarve and Mourne Beg streams. Peat reached European-protected sites downstream.
- June 2022: Planree Ltd pleaded guilty to polluting both streams and was fined €1,500 ([Donegal Live](https://www.donegallive.ie/news/home/871050/anger-at-the-low-fine-for-the-catastrophic-ecological-disaster-at-meenbog.html)).
- April 2024: in proceedings brought by Donegal County Council, the High Court restrained Planree and Mid-Cork Electrical from completing the wind farm. It found the whole wind farm was unauthorised development because of 25 material deviations from its permission. The judge noted both sides agreed there was no evidence that negligence or the deviations caused the slide ([Irish Times, 11 Apr 2024](https://irishtimes.com/crime-law/courts/2024/04/11/council-secures-court-order-prohibiting-further-development-at-wind-farm-where-bog-slide-occurred)).
- Indicative score under the current method: water 12 (court case over discharge to water), enforcement 5 (one prosecution plus one court stop order), habitat 12 to 15 (judgement), climate 0. About 29 to 32, **Elevated**.
- **Decision for the maintainer:** listing a renewable-energy developer is a policy choice as well as a data one. The evidence is solid. If added, the entry should state that the court found no evidence the deviations caused the slide.

## 3. River pollution (Inland Fisheries Ireland prosecutions)

**North Cork Co-operative Creamery (existing entry).** Mallow District Court, April 2026: pleaded guilty to two offences (Local Government (Water Pollution) Act 1977 s.3(1), Fisheries (Consolidation) Act 1959 s.17) over a 22 June 2025 discharge from its wastewater treatment plant into the River Allow at Kanturk. €4,000 fine on each charge plus €3,650 costs. The riverbed was coated in sewage fungus for 450 m; no fish were killed by this discharge. The EPA had suspended its licence to discharge to the Allow. The company said it has invested €1m in remedial works ([Irish Times, 21 Apr 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/ireland/2026/04/21/dairy-processor-fined-for-illegal-effluent-discharge-into-blackwater-river-tributary/)).
- **Proposed:** add evidence; prosecutions 1 → 2. Score 46 → 49 (stays High). Gains the "Repeat offender" badge: both cases ended in guilty pleas and fines, so the label is supported.
- **Must not say:** that it caused the August 2025 Blackwater fish kill. The company stated "no causal link" in court, and see section 5.

**Tirlán (existing entry, Virginia).** Monaghan District Court, April 2025: pleaded guilty to a 24 November 2023 pollution event on a tributary of the Knappagh River (River Erne system), a wild brown trout spawning river. The court applied the Probation Act on condition of a €5,000 donation to a local charity; Tirlán paid €15,177 in costs ([Inland Fisheries Ireland, 29 Apr 2025](https://www.fisheriesireland.ie/news/media-releases/ifi-welcomes-court-ruling-regarding-tirlan-water-pollution-incident-in-co)).
- **Proposed:** add evidence; prosecutions 0 → 1; water 0 → 12 (court case over discharges to water). Score about 12 → 27 (Watch → Elevated). The validator will flag this as a large score change, as it should.
- **For the lawyer:** under the Probation Act a case can be dismissed without a conviction being recorded. The method counts "concluded prosecutions", and Coillte's Glenaboy case is already counted the same way. Wording must say "pleaded guilty; Probation Act applied", never "convicted".
- Note: the Tirlán incident was at a Monaghan site, but our Tirlán entry is mapped to Virginia, Co Cavan. Either add the site or confirm which facility it was before mapping it.

**Uisce Éireann (existing entry).** Further IFI convictions exist (for example the Sullane River in Cork with Glanua Ireland). Its prosecutions count (35) is already well past the enforcement cap, so these add nothing to the score. Optional evidence only.

## 4. Peat extraction

- The EPA found illegal large-scale peat extraction at 38 sites in 2025 ([Irish Times, 26 Jun 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/environment/2025/06/26/illegal-peat-harvesting-still-taking-place-on-large-scale-epa-report-finds/)).
- In June 2026 the European Commission referred Ireland to the Court of Justice for failing to stop illegal peat extraction. About 370,000 tonnes a year (about €40m) is exported without the required licences. The EPA passed dossiers on 38 sites to seven councils (Offaly, Westmeath, Roscommon, Tipperary, Longford, Kildare, Sligo) and no enforcement followed ([Irish Times, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.irishtimes.com/environment/2026/06/04/eu-is-taking-ireland-to-court-over-failure-to-stop-illegal-peat-extraction/)).
- Operators are not named in that reporting. The EPA's High Court case against Harte Peat Ltd was reversed by the Court of Appeal in 2024 (no legal basis for the injunction, as the area was under 50 ha when proceedings began). **Do not list Harte Peat.**
- **Proposed:** a context fact on the page. Possible future work: the councils' enforcement records, if any are published.

## 5. Blackwater fish kill, August 2025: do not attribute

Between 32,000 and 42,000 fish died on the Munster Blackwater in August 2025. The EPA's investigation (41 inspections, 40 samples) found no causal link to any EPA-regulated site, wastewater plant or drinking water plant. The inter-agency group's most likely explanation was an irritant whose source could not be identified ([EPA report](https://www.epa.ie/publications/monitoring--assessment/freshwater--marine/epa-report-blackwater-fish-mortality-investigation-july--august-2025.php)). Linking it to any polluter on the board, including North Cork, would be unsupported and a defamation risk.

## 6. State of protected habitats

NPWS Article 17 report, December 2025: 90% of Ireland's EU-protected habitats are in unfavourable status (48% bad, 42% inadequate), up from 85% in 2019; 51% are still declining. The most common pressure is agriculture (grazing, drainage, manure and fertiliser pollution), followed by invasive species, development, forestry, roads and changes to rivers ([Irish Times, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.irishtimes.com/environment/2025/12/11/irelands-natural-habitats-decline-further-with-90-officially-in-poor-condition/)).
- **Proposed:** a new headline fact ("90% of protected habitats in unfavourable status, 2025") and a line on the method page.
